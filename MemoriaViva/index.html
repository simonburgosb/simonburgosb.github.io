PROJECT OVERVIEW

Bringing History to Life Through Interaction

Memoria Viva is an interdisciplinary project that combines user research, interaction design and technology to create meaningful experiences that encourage visitors to discover the history of "Las Matronas" at Santa Fe de Antioquia in a more engaging, memorable and participatory way.

Duration

One academic semester

Team

Multidisciplinary team

My Role

Project Lead · UX Research · Interaction Design · Development

Deliverables

Landing Page + Three Interactive Products

THE CHALLENGE

Making Cultural Heritage More Engaging

Problem

Traditional museum experiences often rely on passive observation, making it difficult to attract younger audiences or encourage meaningful interaction with historical content.

Opportunity

By combining physical artifacts with interactive technologies, visitors could actively participate in discovering stories instead of simply reading them.

Goal

Design an ecosystem of interactive experiences capable of increasing engagement, curiosity and emotional connection with the cultural heritage of Santa Fe de Antioquia.

MY ROLE

Leading Research and Shaping the Experience

As the project leader, I coordinated the development of the experience while actively participating throughout the entire design process. My primary contribution focused on UX research, including secondary research, interviews, surveys and the synthesis of qualitative findings into actionable insights.

Based on these findings, I helped define interaction concepts, user flows and product architecture. I also collaborated on wireframing, interface design, prototyping and supported the programming of the interactive experiences, ensuring that research decisions were reflected in the final products.

RESEARCH PROCESS

Understanding People Before Designing Solutions

Every design decision started with understanding people rather than defining solutions. We explored how visitors interacted with cultural spaces, what captured their attention and which obstacles prevented meaningful engagement with historical content.

By combining secondary research, interviews, surveys and collaborative synthesis, we transformed raw observations into actionable insights that guided every interactive experience presented in this project.

01

Secondary Research

We analyzed museum case studies, interactive exhibitions and inclusive design projects to understand current approaches to cultural engagement and interactive storytelling.

02

Interviews

Semi-structured interviews allowed us to understand how different users perceive historical experiences, what they remember or know aboit "Las Matronas" and which interactions could generate stronger emotional connections.

03

Surveys

Surveys complemented qualitative findings by helping us validate recurring behaviors, expectations and preferences across a larger number of participants.

04

Synthesis

Jouney Map, clustering and discussions transformed raw information into pain points, insights and design opportunities that guided every following design decision.

DESIGN DECISIONS

Turning Research Into Design Decisions

Every design decision emerged directly from our research findings. Instead of adding technology for its own sake, each interaction was intentionally designed to solve a real user need identified during the investigation phase.

01

Research Insight

People connect better with history when they actively participate instead of passively consuming information.

Design Response

Every product was designed around active exploration, encouraging visitors to discover stories through interaction rather than observation.

02

Research Insight

Curiosity increases when visitors manipulate physical objects that reveal hidden stories.

Design Response

Physical interaction became the trigger for revealing digital narratives, strengthening engagement with museum artifacts.

03

Research Insight

Shared experiences generate stronger emotional memories than individual interactions.

Design Response

Collaborative mechanics were incorporated to encourage conversations between visitors and create collective experiences.

04

Research Insight

Simple interfaces reduce cognitive load and encourage exploration across different age groups.

Design Response

Interfaces were simplified to let users focus on the historical experience instead of learning complex interactions.

SOLUTION

Designing an Interactive Ecosystem

Rather than designing a single installation, we created an ecosystem of three complementary experiences. Each product addressed a different way of interacting with cultural heritage while sharing the same visual language, narrative and educational purpose.

Together, these experiences encourage visitors to explore, collaborate and discover historical stories from multiple perspectives. Every interaction was intentionally designed to transform passive observation into active participation.

PRODUCT 01

Maxilibro

A large interactive storybook that transforms traditional reading into a collaborative experience. Visitors physically interact with the book to unlock historical narratives, illustrations and multimedia content.

The goal was to encourage curiosity and discussion while making historical information easier to understand through interaction instead of passive reading.

PRODUCT 02

Memory Map

An interactive map that allows visitors to explore Santa Fe de Antioquia through locations and stories, revealing hidden narratives as they navigate the experience. Letting them Understand the history of multiple "Matronas"

PRODUCT 03

Chismógrafo

Inspired by traditional gossip books, this interactive experience invites visitors to tell their stories, their most epic stories and compare their responses with stories form other people in Santa Fe de Antioquia.

The experience promotes social interaction, conversation and emotional engagement by connecting personal memories with collective cultural heritage.

DESIGN PROCESS

From Research to Interactive Experiences

Every design decision was driven by research findings. Rather than jumping directly into visual solutions, we progressively transformed insights into concepts, interaction flows and functional prototypes that could be validated throughout the project.

01

Ideation

Using research insights as our foundation, we explored multiple concepts through brainstorming, sketching sessions and collaborative workshops to identify the most promising interaction ideas.

02

User Flows

Interaction flows were mapped to ensure each experience remained intuitive while maintaining consistency across the three products.

03

Wireframes

Low-fidelity wireframes allowed the team to define layouts, navigation and interaction logic before investing time in visual refinement.

04

Visual Design

Interfaces and physical components were designed to reinforce the project's identity while keeping interactions clear, accessible and engaging.

05

Development

Interactive prototypes were implemented through a combination of software and physical computing, ensuring every interaction behaved as intended.

REFLECTION

How This Project Shaped My Design Process

Leading Memoria Viva while contributing to research, interaction design, prototyping and development completely changed the way I approach digital experiences. Instead of beginning with technology, I now start by understanding people, identifying meaningful opportunities and allowing research to guide every design decision.

Research Before Design

Conducting interviews, surveys and qualitative analysis showed me that the strongest ideas rarely come from assumptions. Understanding people first consistently leads to better design decisions.

Designing as a Team

Leading a multidisciplinary team reinforced the importance of communication, iteration and shared ownership. The best solutions emerged when every discipline contributed throughout the design process.

Technology Needs Purpose

This project confirmed that technology should never become the center of the experience. Its value lies in strengthening stories, emotions and human interaction rather than competing with them.
